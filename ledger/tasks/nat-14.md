- **Windows x64 only.** The native tests ran on Windows x64 (hosted and local); Linux and macOS compilation of the native sources was not run, and the helper cannot load the library on Linux or macOS (`NativeLoader` is win-x64 only), so the production helper fails closed there.

- The dotnet mutants were not run by anyone; the architecture and package-validation tests were observed only in hosted CI.

## Obligations carried to NAT.15 (recorded from the review; copy into the NAT.15 claim note)

- The drain wait in `handle_table::close` protects the lifetime of the caller's `arc_io_v1` context, and no test checks it (removing it survives even under ASan stress). NAT.15 adds a test that the in-flight read callback count is zero when `arc_pdf_close` returns.
- The box-cut and straddle loops are O(boxes) per iteration and carry no deadline check; the box count comes from the backend. NAT.15 bounds or checks them.
- A real backend must read the input in a loop with a progress guard and call the engine's deadline check regularly (deadlines are cooperative; `arc_pdf_close` blocks until a running call returns); scripts, actions and file-system font access must be off inside the sandbox; a box wider than a whole window, or beginning before a caller-chosen start, is carried by no chunk.

## Completion

NAT.14 is **complete** as narrowed by the merged planning repair (Design #227 / Plan #293): its outcome and acceptance are the engine, the managed binding and the production composition seam behind a backend interface with fake-backend containment tests, and that is merged, reviewed and green. The real-library obligations that the original task text named are owned by NAT.15, not by NAT.14: the real PDFium build and binding, packaged PDF fixtures, malformed/native-crash/hang/parent-death acceptance against the real library on every admitted RID, the image-parser containment rerun and the composed `ContentSandbox.Runtime.<rid>` input.
