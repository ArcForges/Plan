## Untested coverage

- **Linux and macOS real-clock and real-stream runs: not performed.** The hosted Linux job runs the offline suite on a manual clock for the limits; the opt-in OS checks are Windows only. macOS has no host and no CI.
- **A second OS account (cross-account denial): never available.**
- **Native AOT publish of `ArcForges.LocalRpc`: not performed.** Static trim and AOT analyzers pass; CI compiles only the PRF.04 probe.
- **The AF_UNIX-on-Windows non-close flake reported at PLT.09 was not root-caused.** The new handshake limit closes such a connection within 10 s, which is not evidence the flake is gone; the opt-in runs above saw no failure.
- **Cancellation and health are proven only through the hand-written test service.** PLT.14 and PLT.15 must re-prove cancellation (and PLT.15 transfer cancellation) through their real contract methods, declared with `RegisterControl`, and can read `GetBoundsSnapshot().ControlAdmitted` as evidence that the call used a reserved slot. Nothing is protected until an owner declares its control methods.
- **Per-connection bounds only:** the connection bound of 8 multiplies them; there is no client-side limit on outgoing calls and no per-caller accounting inside a connection. A peer that makes one cheap call every 59 s keeps its connection slot.
- **Streaming calls and multi-megabyte responses to a peer that stops reading: not tested** (no streaming method exists in the pinned contracts and the probe services cannot produce a multi-megabyte response). A call to an unknown service is answered without admission.
- **Launcher-supplied streams:** the handshake clock starts when a stream is accepted; a launcher that supplies a stream before its child is ready must raise `HandshakeTimeout`.
- **The callback marker is cooperative and process-wide** (one `AsyncLocal`): a handler that calls an unrelated second child is treated as a callback and never queued there; a hostile peer that omits the marker is held only to the data bounds, deadlines and connection limits.
- **The Named Pipe is reachable through the network namespace** and peer OS identity is not read here (inherited from PLT.09 and PLT.10).
- **Not in scope and not claimed:** routing and version refusal (PLT.12), registration lease and fencing (PLT.11), disconnect and retry semantics (PLT.14), brokered resources (PLT.15), package publication (PLT.16), restricted-launch containment (PLT.45).
